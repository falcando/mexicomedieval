Scope and method

Invitation is not a uniform legal category

Roman authorities could admit petitioning populations, recruit auxiliaries, accept a foreign ruler at court, allot provincial land, or commission military action against an opponent. A treaty did not necessarily create territorial settlement, and the modern generic label foederati should not be imposed on evidence describing different arrangements.

The unit of inquiry is a claim about Roman agency, not every movement of foreign people. Particularly, the Vandal crossing in 429 and the sack of Rome in 455 are historical events, but the alleged invitations by Bonifatius and Licinia Eudoxia must be assessed independently. These are included as unproven accusations, not positive examples. The numerical distribution of this deliberately selected preview is not evidence for changing rates of invitation through time.

Ancient text references identify passages for verification. A critical-edition-level audit, manuscript variants and source-dependence analysis are incomplete; this page must not be cited as a definitive critical corpus.
