# Invitations to Empire: editorial methodology and source review

**Version:** 0.1.0 · **Date:** 8 November 2026  
**Status:** Curated research preview. No DOI. Not peer reviewed. Critical-edition collation remains incomplete.

## Research question

When did named Roman authorities allow, invite, commission, finance, settle or otherwise facilitate foreign armed presence within claimed Roman territory? The data do not presuppose that every armed arrival was authorised or that every foreign group was a legally uniform category of *foederati*.

## Record identity and scope

Each of the twenty records identifies a *claim about Roman action*, rather than a migration automatically interpreted as a Roman invitation. Explicit authorisation is separated from settlement, military aid, court reception, commissions to foreign rulers and unverified retrospective accusations. Republican precedents are included, but comparison does not imply the existence of one stable institution from Caesar to Maurice. Dates may be approximate.

**The selection of 20 is purposive**, designed to illustrate different source types and mechanisms. It must not be used to compute historical frequencies. The larger 66-entry working inventory is a research backlog, not a validated list of 66 proven invitations.

## Evidence status

- **attested:** Ancient evidence explicitly narrates or records the relevant Roman act, although its terms, numbers or surrounding interpretations can remain debated.
- **contested:** The underlying interaction or physical presence is documented, but the institutional mechanism, precise details or interpretation are disputed.
- **allegation:** An invitation is asserted, alleged or rumoured in surviving narratives without sufficient basis to describe it as a demonstrated historical summons.

Bonifatius and Licinia Eudoxia must remain in the **allegation** class: the Vandals crossed into Africa in 429 and entered Rome in 455; neither fact independently establishes the two purported invitations. For Eudoxia, consult Hydatius, *Chronicle* **160** in R. W. Burgess's numbering. An earlier research list cited **167**: this has been corrected, rather than silently passed over. Procopius' elaboration is later and needs criticism of literary purpose and source dependence.

## Terminology

Do not conflate *foedus*, *foederati*, *laeti*, military recruitment, land assignment, negotiated surrender, hostages, judicial authority and the independent military intervention of a foreign king. A “Roman invitation” is an analytical research descriptor, not a reconstructed late Roman juridical category. Roman authority includes emperors, magistrates, commanders and, explicitly tagged, usurpers or local urban delegates. A legitimate emperor and a usurper are not institutionally equivalent.

## Witness review protocol

1. Locate the earliest surviving testimony and record its date, genre, language, position and independent relationship to later sources.
2. Check the relevant passage in a *named critical edition*, with variants and alternative numbering recorded where consequential. The present web links are navigation aids, **not certification that an edition has been collated**.
3. Separate the factual occurrence of movement, the invitation claim, the identity of the Roman initiator, its legal authority, the physical destination and the agreed obligations.
4. Compare near-contemporary and later testimony. Silence in a witness is not proof of falsity, but it is also not independent corroboration.
5. Distinguish historical claim, ancient allegation and a modern inference in every annotation.
6. Add original-language lexical evidence when collating the editions: *recipere*, *suscipere*, *transducere*, *dare*, *petere*, *auxilia*, *foedus*, *invitare* where actually present. Do not retrofit a word to an author.
7. Preserve editorial correction logs and explicit doubts. Dates and coordinates must not give an illusion of greater certainty than the evidence permits.

## Integration and publication

The website source is on `dev`, while `main` is automatically generated and serves the public GitHub Pages site. This work is staged on a feature branch to permit review. The source files live in `landing/public/recursos/invitations-to-empire/`; the Next.js static-export workflow must be tested before any merge to `dev`. No DOI should be inserted before an actual authorised deposit.

**Editorial gate before release:** passage-by-passage critical-edition checks, link check, independent historiographical review, suitable open licence, verified authorship/citation, accessibility and build QA. No original-language extracts or modern translations have been reproduced wholesale in this preview.
