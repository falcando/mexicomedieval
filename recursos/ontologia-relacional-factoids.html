Prosopography, documentary relationships and the semantic representation of historical sources

From documentary testimony to historical relationships

Historical prosopography involves more than assembling the attributes of individuals identified in documentary sources. It also requires us to examine the relationships recorded between them, distinguish their different forms and establish the evidential circumstances of each statement. This resource develops a relational factoid model that makes such connections explicit without automatically treating a documentary assertion as an established historical fact.

The model and its origins

The relational model was developed by Hervin Fernández Aceves through research on the documentary evidence of the Norman kingdom of Sicily and subsequently on medieval Sardinian sources. It draws on factoid-based prosopography and extends John Bradley's Factoid Prosopography Ontology (FPO), preserving the distinction between sources, documentary references, actors and relationships.

Rather than assigning an unqualified relationship directly between two individuals, the model represents the assertion recorded by a source, its textual location and the roles of its participants. Documentary evidence, its formal representation and the historian's interpretation therefore remain distinct.

RDF, OWL and ways of representing data

The Resource Description Framework (RDF) represents information through explicit subject–predicate–object statements, or triples. RDF Schema (RDFS) and the Web Ontology Language (OWL) define classes, properties and relationships that give those statements a more precise meaning. Turtle is a compact syntax for writing RDF, while RDF/XML expresses the same graph using XML.

The repository provides two equivalent serialisations of the ontology: an RDF/XML document (.rdf) and a Turtle document (.ttl). It also includes SHACL validation shapes, a SPARQL query and fictitious test data. It is not yet a populated prosopographical database of authentic Sardinian documentary evidence.

Current status

This is an initial working version. Its structure and examples are available for consultation and review, but the model has yet to be tested systematically against historical documents and validated through full reasoning over the combined ontology. Subsequent changes will be documented on GitHub.
